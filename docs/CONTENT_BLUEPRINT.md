# CCT Info Hub — Content Blueprint

## Purpose

The app brings casualty assessment, immediate care, equipment, movement and response information into short connected pages. It is designed for quick browsing and search rather than long-form reading.

## Page pattern

Each practical page should answer the same questions where they apply:

1. What am I looking for?
2. What should I do first?
3. What should I avoid?
4. What change makes this more urgent?
5. What needs to be reassessed?
6. Which related page should I open next?

Equipment pages also cover identification, setup, use checks and common errors. Product links are included only when they make the page more useful.

Write for casualty clearers from non-medical trades, including cooks, clerks and supply technicians. Use plain language, action-first steps and only the technical terms needed to identify equipment or report findings. Pages should work as quick response cards, not exam study.

Do not ask casualty clearers to diagnose a condition, select a medication, calculate a dose or perform a procedure beyond their training. A page may tell them to help a person use their own prescribed rescue inhaler or auto-injector when that assistance is standard first aid, but it must make the boundary explicit.

## App sections

### Assessment and MARCHE

- Scene survey and responsiveness.
- MARCHE from massive bleeding through everything else.
- Rapid body survey, SAMPLE history and vital signs.
- Focused and head-to-toe examination.
- Reassessment, treatment checks and MIST handover.

### Bleeding control

- Recognizing life-threatening bleeding.
- Direct pressure, wound packing and pressure dressings.
- Limb bleeding and tourniquet use.
- Pelvic-injury recognition and bleeding-control reassessment.

### Airway and breathing

- Airway recognition and positioning.
- Adult choking.
- Breathing assessment and support.
- OPA, NPA, i-gel and BVM information.
- Oxygen, open chest wounds and smoke-inhalation concerns.
- Airway and breathing reassessment.

### Circulation and resuscitation

- Pulse and skin signs.
- Shock recognition and support.
- Adult cardiac arrest, CPR and AED use.
- Monitoring and circulation reassessment.

### Head, temperature and burns

- Head-injury concerns and focused head checks.
- Heat-loss prevention, cold exposure and heat illness.
- Thermal, chemical and electrical burns.
- Chemical eye exposure.
- Facial burns and smoke inhalation.

### Splinting and movement

- Injury assessment and distal circulation, sensation and movement checks.
- SAM Splint applications and other support options.
- Traction-splint recognition.
- Casualty preparation, packaging, team movement and post-move reassessment.

### Equipment

- Response-bag check.
- Bleeding, airway and resuscitation equipment.
- Oxygen, AED and basic monitoring equipment.
- Splints, warming equipment and basket-stretcher recognition.

### Response scenarios and communications

- Damage-control and isolated-casualty responses.
- Person-overboard response.
- Firefighter rehabilitation.
- Two-person team roles, radio reports and movement decisions.
- Continued care and handover.

### Sudden illness

The section currently covers:

- Chest pain and suspected heart attack.
- Stroke recognition.
- Seizure care.
- Severe allergic reaction and anaphylaxis.
- Asthma and acute breathing difficulty.

The next bundle should add:

- Diabetic emergencies.
- Poisoning, opioid overdose and naloxone awareness.
- Fainting and unexplained altered responsiveness.

Each topic should connect back to MARCHE, vital signs, reassessment, equipment and handover rather than creating a separate assessment system.

## Remaining equipment and scenario information

After the remaining sudden-illness topics, the most useful additions are:

- Suction setup and use checks.
- Scoop stretcher, KED, Med Sled and backboard/head-bed recognition.
- A concise Propaq/ZOLL monitor page.
- Rad-57 carbon-monoxide monitoring limitations.
- Environmental spill and contaminated-casualty response.
- Carbon-fibre contamination awareness.

## Deliberately excluded

- Patient records or personal-data entry.
- Course schedules, attendance, testing and instructor administration.
- Procurement lists, prices and vendors.
- Full equipment manuals copied into the app.
- Advanced medication, invasive procedure, laboratory or surgical material.
- Duplicate pages that repeat an existing treatment sequence.
- Decorative material that does not improve understanding.

## Completion standard

A content group is complete when its pages are searchable, connected to the relevant assessment and reassessment pages, free of broken internal links, readable on phone and desktop, and included in a successful production build.
