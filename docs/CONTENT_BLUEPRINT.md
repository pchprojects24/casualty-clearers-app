# Casualty Clearers App — Consolidated Content Blueprint

Curated working structure for gathering and organizing the app’s content

## Purpose and direction

The app will bring useful casualty-clearing information together in one clear, easy-to-browse place for AOPS casualty clearers. It will not manage patients, run a course, reproduce every supplied document, or become an administrative system. Source material is raw input. Each item will be judged on whether it helps the intended user understand casualty-clearing responsibilities, processes, skills, situations or equipment.

## Working rules

- Include material because it is useful, not merely because it appears in a source.

- A draft, older or differently written source is analyzed for useful content; its status does not automatically include or exclude it.

- Leave out course schedules, timings, attendance, assessments, instructor administration, procurement details and other administrative material.

- Do not create patient-entry, patient-record or casualty-tracking functions. Communications, plotting and documentation may be explained as subject matter only.

- Missing photographs, manuals or illustrations do not stop progress. If something useful appears later, it can be added later.

- Equipment pages use short practical explanations. Full manufacturer manuals are not required for the app.

- Avoid illustrations that add no useful information or belong only to the original course or document layout.

- General RCN or other-platform material is included only when it genuinely helps the AOPS casualty clearer. It must not displace the known AOPS workflow.

- Repeated material will be combined into the clearest version instead of copied several times.

## Source-by-source decisions

### CCT course material

Use as the primary pool for taught casualty assessment, interventions, equipment, movement and scenario content. Extract the useful subject matter and disregard course administration, lesson sequence, timings and delivery instructions.

### CFCD 136 casualty-clearing manual

Use the sections that explain casualty-clearing organization and practical operation. Strong candidates include team roles, closing up, rapid survey, dispatch, communications, MIST, PACE, casualty assessment, triage, plotting concepts, movement, packaging, firefighter monitoring and special shipboard situations. Exclude distribution material, amendment instructions, empty annex placeholders and platform-specific detail that does not help the AOPS user.

### Major Medical Equipment Core spreadsheet

Use only to identify equipment relevant to casualty clearers. Do not reproduce the full inventory, stock numbers, prices, accounting codes or equipment intended only for advanced sick-bay, laboratory or surgical use.

### Sick Bay acquisition request

Do not use as general app content. The manikins, suppliers, prices and subscription request are procurement and training-support information rather than casualty-clearer subject matter.

### Photos and original material

No photographs are currently available, and none are required to continue. The app structure will work without a photo collection.

### Legacy or rarely used stretchers

- Ashton Waters stretcher: exclude because it is no longer used.

- Stokes or basket stretcher: mention briefly so users recognize the term and general type, but do not give it a large section because it is not normally used.

- Current casualty-movement equipment: give priority to the devices and methods actually taught or carried on the AOPS.

## Proposed app content structure

### 1. Casualty Clearing on AOPS

- Who casualty clearers are and how their role fits with Sick Bay, the medical team and damage control.

- CCT organization, headquarters and two-person teams.

- Emergency-stations muster at Sick Bay.

- Team preparation, equipment, PPE and flash gear.

- Computer/logging and communications roles.

- Dispatch, return to Sick Bay and assistance with continuing monitoring.

- Differences between the known AOPS workflow and generic examples found in other sources.

### 2. Primary Survey — MARCHE

- Scene and hazard check before approaching or treating.

- Massive hemorrhage.

- Airway.

- Respiration.

- Circulation.

- Hypothermia and head injury considerations.

- Everything else and rapid body survey.

- Clear links from each MARCHE component to the practical interventions casualty clearers are trained to perform.

- Communication points during the assessment: identity, location, findings, treatment and priority.

### 3. Secondary Survey

- When the secondary survey begins.

- Systematic head-to-toe assessment.

- History and symptoms appropriate to the casualty-clearer role.

- Vital signs and reassessment.

- Recognition of change or deterioration.

- Information needed for MIST and handover.

### 4. Clinical Skills and Procedures

- Direct pressure and hemorrhage control.

- Tourniquet application.

- Wound packing when part of the taught skill set.

- Basic airway positioning and manoeuvres.

- OPA and NPA when part of the taught skill set.

- Oxygen setup and delivery.

- CPR and AED.

- Burn care.

- Shock recognition and basic care.

- Hypothermia, immersion and cold-water considerations.

- Smoke-inhalation considerations.

- Monitoring and basic vital signs.

### 5. Splinting and Immobilization

- SAM splint.

- Sager traction splint.

- Military traction splint.

- Pelvic binder.

- General splinting principles.

- Checks before and after application.

- Preparing the casualty for movement.

### 6. Casualty Movement

- Choosing whether and when to move a casualty.

- Preparing and packaging a casualty.

- Team roles, commands and communication during movement.

- Movement through passageways, hatches, ladders and constrained spaces.

- Stretcher recognition and use.

- Movement involving rescue boats, recovery points or aircraft when relevant to the AOPS context.

- A brief Stokes or basket-stretcher mention without treating it as a routinely used device.

### 7. Communications and Handover

- Initial report when a casualty is found.

- MIST.

- PACE communications.

- SITREP and SIOR concepts where relevant.

- Priority and triage terminology.

- Communication between the casualty team, Sick Bay and the medical team.

- Plotting and casualty-information concepts as instructions only, with no patient-data function in the app.

- Handover and reassessment updates.

### 8. AOPS-Specific Situations

- Emergency stations for fire or flood.

- Isolated casualty response.

- Person-overboard response and the casualty-clearer role in the rescue boat.

- Recovery and transfer back into the ship.

- Cold-water and immersion exposure.

- Firefighter rehabilitation, cooling chairs and firefighter vital signs.

- Sustained-fire response.

- Smoke and carbon-monoxide exposure.

- Carbon-fibre contamination and decontamination.

- HAZMAT and CBRN awareness appropriate to the casualty-clearer role.

- Emergency flying stations or crash-on-deck considerations when applicable.

- Other situations only when the material gives casualty clearers something useful to recognize or do.

### 9. Equipment Quick Use

This section will cover equipment casualty clearers may actually handle. It will not reproduce full manuals. Each item will receive the same compact pattern:

- What it is.

- What it is used for.

- Basic setup.

- Simple steps for use.

- Checks during use.

- Common mistakes or cautions that are useful to the casualty clearer.

- What to do with it after use.

- Links to the assessment, procedure or movement page where it belongs.

Initial equipment candidates include oxygen equipment, AED, tourniquets, airway adjuncts, SAM splint, traction splints, pelvic binder, casualty-movement devices, basic vital-sign equipment, Propaq/ZOLL, Rad-57, warming equipment, suction and resuscitation equipment. The inventory will be filtered before any equipment page is written.

### 10. Terms and Source Notes

Keep the user-facing material clean. A short glossary may explain terms such as CCT, SMA, MIST, MARCHE, PACE, SMART triage and SIOR. Source tracking can remain behind the content so material can be traced without turning the app into an administrative library.

## Content deliberately excluded or reduced

- Course schedules and lesson administration.

- Attendance, testing, instructor duties and sign-off processes.

- Procurement prices, vendors and purchase requests.

- The complete ship medical-equipment inventory.

- Advanced clinical, laboratory, surgical or sick-bay equipment outside the casualty-clearer role.

- Ashton Waters stretcher material.

- Large Stokes-stretcher coverage.

- Blank forms and empty document placeholders.

- Decorative, duplicated or course-layout illustrations that do not improve understanding.

- Generic platform procedures that conflict with or distract from the AOPS workflow.

## How additional material will be handled

New files can be added at any time. Each one will be reviewed for useful facts, procedures, equipment information, terminology or AOPS context. Useful material will be placed in the appropriate topic, repeated material will be merged, irrelevant material will be dropped, and missing material will simply remain open until something worthwhile is supplied.

## iCloud Drive source review

### Selected material

- CRR 5-90 CCOTT AOPV Controlled Revision 6: selected as a high-value working source because its 89 pages contain detailed AOPS organization, readiness, assessment, equipment, movement, communications, isolated-casualty and spill-response content. Course maps and delivery administration remain excluded.

- CRR 5-93 Environmental Spill Medical Response: selected for AOPS spill response with and without casualties, equipment taken to the scene and prepared to receive, Hot/Warm/Cold Zone concepts, casualty flow, PPE, doffing and decontamination.

- Station and practical cards plus the individual skills checklist: the forms themselves are not app content, but they clearly identify what casualty clearers perform, what they practise as a group and what is familiarization only. That distinction will guide page depth.

- Propaq MD Quick Reference Guide: selected only as background for a short Propaq setup and monitoring page. The full guide will not be reproduced.

- AOPS and HDW At Sea Medical Capabilities variants: selected only for useful, non-duplicated material such as cold-water immersion stages and practical recognition topics. Medical administration and unrelated ship-class material are excluded.

### Practical scope clarified by the iCloud material

- Individually performed content: scene size-up, AVPU, MARCHE, MIST, OPA, NPA, BVM, oxygen-cylinder setup and shutdown, respirations, pulse, capillary refill, manual blood pressure, assigned Propaq readings, direct pressure, pressure dressing, wound packing, tourniquet application and reassessment, arm splinting with circulation/sensation/movement checks, and AED power/pads/clear/prompts.

- Training-only line preparation: spiking a training IV-fluid bag and priming the administration line. The source expressly excludes IV insertion and fluid administration.

- Group movement and packaging content: Scoop, KED, Med Sled, backboard with and without head bed, plus brief cervical-collar demonstration.

- Familiarization-level material: CCT first-aid bag, Cocoon and SAGER. These should receive recognition and simple-use coverage rather than the same depth as individually performed skills.

### Material not selected

- The broad ACTT advanced-care library, medication material, invasive procedures and clinician-level trauma study material: wrong audience and outside the casualty-clearer role.

- The 2017 airway-management reference card: focused on intubation, advanced airway assessment and medications rather than casualty-clearer skills.

- The full 388-page Philips HeartStart MRx manual: excessive and not the clearest match for the equipment emphasis already identified.

- The one-page oxygen-cylinder duration table: a calculation reference rather than a simple equipment-use explanation.

- The older British Columbia hypothermia clinical guideline and submersion-evacuation flowchart: mainly clinician and transport-system material. Useful casualty-clearer concepts are already better represented in the AOPS course sources.

- Exact duplicate copies of CRR 5-92, 5-93, 5-94 and 5-95: no reason to preserve multiple identical copies in the project repository.

- Course schedules, run sheets, assessment administration and instructor-delivery paperwork: no app role.

### Blueprint changes from this review

- Add CCT first-aid bag familiarization under Equipment Quick Use.

- Make Scoop, KED, Med Sled and backboard/head-bed the main movement-device pages.

- Keep Cocoon and SAGER at familiarization depth unless later material shows a greater casualty-clearer role.

- Add a dedicated environmental-spill casualty-response topic under AOPS-Specific Situations.

- Create a concise Propaq page focused on powering the unit, selecting the appropriate monitoring view, obtaining assigned readings and reporting the results.

- Use performed, group-practised and familiarization-only distinctions internally to determine how much detail each subject receives.

- Retain cold-water immersion stages as potentially useful POB context without importing the surrounding medical-administration material.

## Treatment accuracy review — 11 September 2026

A current-source treatment pass is complete and recorded in the separate document “Casualty Clearers App — Treatment Accuracy and Content Decisions” in this project folder.

The review confirms that the core content remains useful, but requires several controlled updates before app-ready pages are drafted: use the 2025 Heart & Stroke choking sequence; remove the older two-finger infant CPR method; do not present oxygen as automatic treatment; frame collars, KEDs and backboards as locally directed packaging/movement equipment rather than routine spinal immobilization; use current open-chest-wound guidance; record the burn-cooling source difference; and keep pulse-oximetry, Rad-57 and Propaq values within a complete assessment and assigned casualty-clearer role.

The first interactive web-app build was authorized and started on 11 September 2026. GitHub Pages remains disabled until separate publication authorization.
